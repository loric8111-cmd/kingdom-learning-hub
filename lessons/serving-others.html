BIBLE STUDIES • LESSON 09

Serving Others

Discover how following God can be expressed through love, humility, generosity, compassion and practical service to other people.

1. What Does It Mean to Serve?

Serving means using what we have—our time, abilities, resources and opportunities—to help others and contribute to something greater than ourselves.

Christian service is not simply about performing religious activities. It can also involve practical acts of love, kindness, responsibility and compassion.

Key idea:

True service focuses on helping others rather than making ourselves the center of attention.

2. Jesus' Example of Service

Jesus taught and demonstrated servant leadership. He showed that greatness in God's Kingdom is not defined simply by status or power.

Mark 10:45

Jesus explained that He came not to be served, but to serve and to give His life for others.

His example challenges Christians to think beyond personal recognition and ask how their lives can benefit other people.

3. Serving With Humility

Service can lose its meaning when the primary motivation becomes praise, recognition or social status.

Humility means recognizing that serving others does not make us superior to them.

Philippians 2:3–4

Paul encourages believers to act with humility and to consider the interests of others.

4. Different Ways to Serve

🤝

Helping People

Support someone who is struggling through practical help, encouragement or companionship.

❤️

Showing Compassion

Pay attention to people who may be overlooked, isolated or vulnerable.

🎓

Teaching

Use knowledge and skills to help another person learn and develop.

🎁

Giving

Share resources responsibly when you have an opportunity to meet a genuine need.

🙏

Prayer

Pray for people and bring their needs before God.

🛠️

Practical Work

Use your abilities, time and effort to solve problems and support others.

5. Serving the Vulnerable

Scripture repeatedly emphasizes compassion toward people who are vulnerable or in need.

This can include people experiencing poverty, widows, orphans, strangers, the sick and others who may need support.

James 1:27

James connects sincere religious devotion with caring for orphans and widows in their distress and maintaining a life of moral integrity.

Look beyond yourself.

Sometimes service begins simply by noticing a need that other people have ignored.

6. Serving Without Seeking Attention

Not every good action needs to become public. Jesus warned against performing acts of righteousness simply to receive recognition from other people.

Matthew 6:1–4

Jesus taught His followers to avoid turning their acts of generosity into performances for public approval.

The goal is not to hide every good action. Rather, the lesson is to examine the motivation behind the action.

7. Your Abilities Can Serve Others

People have different skills, experiences, resources and opportunities.

One person may be good at teaching. Another may be good at organizing, building, listening, encouraging, creating, managing or solving problems.

Ask Yourself

  • What am I naturally good at?
  • What have I learned that could help someone else?
  • What resources do I have available?
  • What needs do I see around me?
  • How can I use my abilities responsibly?

8. Serving in Ordinary Life

Service does not always require a large platform, organization or public ministry.

It can happen in ordinary situations:

  • Helping someone understand something.
  • Listening to someone who needs encouragement.
  • Helping with practical work.
  • Sharing useful knowledge.
  • Supporting your family responsibly.
  • Helping someone who is struggling.
  • Encouraging someone who has lost hope.

9. Serving Your Community

A Christian can look at their community and ask what positive contribution they can make.

STEP 01

Observe

Pay attention to real needs around you instead of assuming someone else will solve everything.

STEP 02

Understand

Learn what people actually need before deciding how to help.

STEP 03

Act

Take a practical step that is within your ability and resources.

STEP 04

Follow Through

Responsible service means keeping commitments and learning from experience.

10. Generosity

Generosity involves willingly sharing what we have with others when there is a genuine opportunity to help.

2 Corinthians 9:7

Paul teaches that giving should not be done reluctantly or under compulsion, but willingly.

Generosity is not measured only by the amount given. Motivation, wisdom and the genuine needs of others also matter.

11. Serving With Wisdom

Serving others does not mean saying yes to every request or ignoring wisdom and personal responsibilities.

Good service should be responsible, sustainable and directed toward genuine needs.

Compassion needs wisdom.

Before helping, understand the situation, consider what is actually needed and choose a responsible way to contribute.

12. Do Not Become Proud of Service

Doing good things can sometimes create pride. A person may begin comparing their service with other people's service.

Christian service should produce humility rather than superiority.

Luke 17:10

Jesus teaches His followers to maintain humility about their obedience and service.

13. Serving When Nobody Applauds

One of the strongest tests of character is whether we continue doing what is right when there is no public recognition.

A person who serves only when people are watching may be seeking attention rather than genuinely serving.

A Quiet Act of Service

Imagine noticing someone struggling with a task. You help them, but nobody records it, posts about it or praises you.

You still helped.

That is an opportunity to practice service without depending on applause.

14. Service and Leadership

Leadership and service are not opposites. Healthy leadership often means taking responsibility for the wellbeing and progress of others.

John 13:12–15

Jesus used the washing of His disciples' feet as an example of humble service.

15. Avoiding Burnout

Serving others is important, but wisdom also requires recognizing personal limits.

A person who constantly gives without rest, planning or boundaries may eventually become exhausted and unable to serve effectively.

  • Rest when necessary.
  • Share responsibilities.
  • Plan your time.
  • Do not promise what you cannot realistically do.
  • Ask for help when needed.

16. A Simple Service Plan

Create Your Personal Service Plan

Write down:

  • One skill you can use.
  • One person or group you can encourage.
  • One practical need you can help with.
  • One resource you can share responsibly.
  • One way you can serve without seeking recognition.

17. Seven-Day Service Challenge

For the next seven days, intentionally look for one opportunity each day to serve another person.

DAY 01

Encourage

Give someone genuine encouragement.

DAY 02

Help

Offer practical assistance to someone.

DAY 03

Listen

Give someone your attention without rushing them.

DAY 04

Teach

Share something useful that you know.

DAY 05

Give

Share a resource responsibly where there is a genuine need.

DAY 06

Pray

Pray specifically for another person's needs.

DAY 07

Reflect

Consider what you learned about service and your own character.

18. Key Scriptures

  • Mark 10:45 — Jesus' example of service
  • Philippians 2:3–4 — Humility and considering others
  • Matthew 6:1–4 — Giving without seeking attention
  • James 1:27 — Caring for people in distress
  • 2 Corinthians 9:7 — Willing generosity
  • John 13:12–15 — Jesus demonstrates humble service
  • Galatians 5:13 — Serving one another through love

  • What is the relationship between humility and service?

  • How can you serve people without seeking recognition?
  • Why is wisdom important when helping others?
  • What needs do you see in your community?
  • How can you practice generosity responsibly?
  • What does Jesus' example of washing His disciples' feet teach about leadership?
  • What is one practical way you can serve someone this week?
