PRAYER • LESSON 01

What Is Prayer?

Understanding prayer as communication, relationship, dependence and fellowship with God.

Introduction: Prayer Is More Than Asking

Prayer is one of the most important practices in the Christian life. Yet prayer is often misunderstood.

Some people think prayer is simply asking God for things. Others see it as a religious ritual that must be performed using certain words.

Biblically, prayer is much deeper.

Prayer is the believer intentionally turning toward God with worship, thanksgiving, confession, requests, questions, dependence and surrender.

Key Idea Prayer is not simply talking to God about what you want. It is learning to bring your whole life before God.

1. Prayer Is Communication With God

At its most basic level, prayer involves communicating with God.

A Christian can speak to God honestly about what is happening internally and externally.

This includes joy, fear, confusion, gratitude, disappointment, temptation, hope and need.

Prayer therefore gives believers a place to stop pretending that they are self-sufficient.

Real-world example

Imagine that you have an important decision to make. Instead of immediately reacting according to fear or pressure, you stop and bring the situation before God.

You might pray:

“God, I don't know what the best decision is. Give me wisdom and help me act according to truth.”

2. Prayer Is Relationship With God

Prayer is not merely a transaction.

A transaction says:

“I give something, and I receive something in return.”

A relationship is different.

In relationship, communication is valuable because of the person you are communicating with.

Christian prayer is rooted in knowing and approaching God as He has revealed Himself.

This means prayer should gradually become more than a list of requests.

It should become part of walking with God.

3. Jesus Demonstrated the Importance of Prayer

Jesus regularly prayed during His earthly ministry.

“But Jesus often withdrew to lonely places and prayed.”

Luke 5:16

This is significant because Jesus did not treat prayer as an unnecessary activity.

He intentionally created space to withdraw from the crowds and pray.

His example teaches Christians that prayer requires intentionality.

If prayer is always postponed until everything else is finished, it may continually be pushed aside.

4. Prayer Includes Worship

Prayer begins with God, not merely with our problems.

Worship recognizes God's character, authority, goodness, holiness, faithfulness and greatness.

Jesus demonstrated this principle in the model prayer.

“Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name.”

Matthew 6:9

Before asking for daily needs, Jesus directs attention toward God's name and God's kingdom.

This teaches us to remember who God is before focusing only on what we want from Him.

5. Prayer Includes Thanksgiving

Prayer should include gratitude.

It is easy to focus so strongly on what is missing that we forget what we have already received.

Thanksgiving changes the direction of our attention.

“Do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God.”

Philippians 4:6

Notice that thanksgiving does not replace requests. They work together.

We can honestly bring our needs to God while also recognizing His goodness and provision.

6. Prayer Includes Confession

Prayer is also a place for honesty about sin.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us our sins and purify us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Confession means acknowledging our wrongdoing rather than hiding it or making excuses.

A believer can come before God and say:

“God, I was wrong. I recognize what I did. Forgive me, help me understand why I fell into this and give me strength to walk differently.”

Prayer therefore becomes part of repentance and spiritual growth.

7. Prayer Includes Asking

God invites His people to bring their needs before Him.

These needs can include:

  • wisdom
  • strength
  • daily provision
  • guidance
  • protection
  • help during difficulty
  • opportunities to serve others

However, asking God for something does not mean that God becomes obligated to fulfill every request exactly as we imagine.

Christian prayer includes trust in God's wisdom.

8. Prayer Includes Surrender

One of the deepest aspects of prayer is surrender.

Jesus prayed in the context of His suffering:

“Yet not my will, but yours be done.”

Luke 22:42

This demonstrates that prayer can contain strong personal desires while still submitting to God's will.

Surrender does not mean that your desires don't matter. It means recognizing that God's wisdom is greater than your own.

A Mature Prayer “God, this is what I desire. But I trust You enough to submit my desire to Your wisdom and will.”

9. Prayer and Dependence

Human beings naturally want independence.

We often say:

“I can handle it myself.”

Prayer challenges this mindset.

It reminds us that our knowledge, strength and control are limited.

James 1:5 encourages those who lack wisdom to ask God.

Prayer therefore develops dependence without removing responsibility.

We pray for wisdom, and then we use that wisdom to act.

10. Prayer Does Not Replace Action

Prayer should never become an excuse for avoiding responsibility.

Example

If you pray:

“God, help me succeed in my studies,”

you should also study.

If you pray:

“God, help me become financially responsible,”

you should also learn how to manage money.

If you pray:

“God, help me overcome temptation,”

you should also establish wise boundaries.

Prayer and responsible action belong together.

11. Prayer During Difficult Times

Prayer becomes especially important when life becomes difficult.

You may experience:

  • financial pressure
  • failure
  • loss
  • uncertainty
  • rejection
  • conflict
  • fear
  • disappointment

Prayer does not guarantee that every difficult situation will immediately disappear.

But prayer gives us a place to bring our suffering to God and seek His strength, wisdom and peace.

12. Prayer Can Become a Lifestyle

“Pray without ceasing.”

1 Thessalonians 5:17

This does not mean that a Christian must speak audible prayers every second of the day.

It points toward a life characterized by continual dependence upon God.

You can develop small moments of prayer throughout the day.

Morning

“God, guide me today.”

Before a decision

“Give me wisdom.”

During temptation

“Give me strength to choose what is right.”

After success

“Thank You.”

Before sleep

“Thank You for carrying me through today.”

13. What Prayer Is Not

Understanding prayer also requires understanding what prayer is not.

  • Prayer is not a way to manipulate God.
  • Prayer is not a magic formula.
  • Prayer is not a competition.
  • Prayer is not a replacement for responsible action.
  • Prayer is not simply asking for material possessions.
  • Prayer is not about impressing other people.

Jesus warned against praying merely to gain public attention in Matthew 6:5–6.

The purpose of prayer is not to create an impressive religious image.

It is to genuinely turn toward God.

14. A Simple Pattern for Prayer

A useful framework for developing prayer is:

W — Worship Recognize who God is.

C — Confession Honestly acknowledge sin.

T — Thanksgiving Recognize what you are grateful for.

S — Supplication Bring your legitimate needs before God.

I — Intercession Pray for other people.

S — Surrender Submit your desires to God's wisdom and will.

This is not a formula that God requires. It is simply a practical structure that can help organize your prayer life.

15. Building Your First Daily Prayer Habit

You do not need to begin with an extremely long prayer session.

Start consistently.

Morning

Spend a few minutes thanking God, reading Scripture and committing your day to Him.

During the Day

Pause before important decisions and difficult situations.

Evening

Review your day. Ask yourself:

  • Where did I honor God?
  • Where did I fail?
  • What am I thankful for?
  • What do I need to change?
  • What do I need to surrender?

Reflection: What Is My Prayer Life Really Like?

  1. Do I pray only when I have problems?
  2. Do I regularly thank God?
  3. Do I honestly confess my failures?
  4. Do I pray for other people?
  5. Do I seek God's wisdom before important decisions?
  6. Am I willing to say, “Your will be done”?
  7. Does prayer actually affect the way I live?
  8. What practical change can I make to my prayer life?

Final Thought

Prayer is not simply a religious activity that we perform because we are Christians.

It is part of learning to walk with God.

Through prayer we learn to worship, depend, confess, give thanks, ask, intercede, surrender and persevere.

The Goal The goal is not simply to become someone who prays more. The deeper goal is to become someone who walks with God more consciously.

When prayer becomes part of everyday life, it begins to affect how we handle money, temptation, relationships, decisions, failure, success and uncertainty.

Prayer teaches us to bring our lives before God instead of trying to live our lives without Him.
