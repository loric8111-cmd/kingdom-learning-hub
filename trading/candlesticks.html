TRADING ACADEMY • LESSON 04

Candlesticks & Charts

Learn how traders visualize price movement, understand OHLC data and read basic candlestick charts.

1. What Is a Price Chart?

A price chart is a visual representation of how the price of a financial instrument changes over time.

Instead of looking at thousands of individual prices, a trader can use a chart to identify patterns, trends, levels and changes in market behavior.

Key idea:

A chart does not tell you with certainty what will happen next. It shows what price has done and helps traders analyze possible scenarios.

2. What Is a Candlestick?

A candlestick is a way of displaying price information for a specific period of time.

Each candle contains four important prices:

Example candlestick
O Open
H High
L Low
C Close

3. Open, High, Low and Close

The four OHLC values describe what happened during the selected time period.

  • Open: The price at which the period began.
  • High: The highest price reached during the period.
  • Low: The lowest price reached during the period.
  • Close: The price at which the period ended.

Example

Imagine a 1-hour candle has:

Open = 1.1000

High = 1.1050

Low = 1.0980

Close = 1.1030

This tells us the range of prices traded during that one-hour period.

4. Candle Body

The candle body represents the distance between the opening price and closing price.

If the closing price is above the opening price, the candle represents an upward price movement for that period.

If the closing price is below the opening price, the candle represents a downward price movement for that period.

5. Wicks

The thin lines extending above or below the candle body are commonly called wicks, shadows or tails.

They show prices that were reached during the period but were not the opening or closing price.

Think of it this way:

The body shows the relationship between open and close. The wicks show the additional price range visited during that period.

6. Bullish and Bearish Candles

Type Meaning
Bullish Close is above the open.
Bearish Close is below the open.

Charting platforms may use different colors to represent bullish and bearish candles. Always check the chart settings rather than assuming a specific color.

7. Candle Size

The size of a candle can provide information about the amount of price movement during its period.

A relatively large body can indicate substantial movement between the open and close. A small body indicates that the opening and closing prices were relatively close together.

However, candle size should always be considered in context. One candle alone does not provide a complete trading signal.

8. Timeframes

A timeframe determines the amount of time represented by each candle.

Timeframe Each Candle Represents
1 Minute 1 minute
5 Minutes 5 minutes
15 Minutes 15 minutes
1 Hour 1 hour
4 Hours 4 hours
Daily 1 day

9. Why Timeframes Matter

The same market can look completely different depending on the timeframe you use.

For example, a market may appear to be rising on a 5-minute chart while simultaneously being in a broader downward movement on a daily chart.

Remember:

A lower timeframe shows more detailed price movement. A higher timeframe provides a broader view of the market.

10. Reading a Simple Candle

Example Scenario

EUR/USD opens at 1.1000.

During the period it reaches 1.1040.

It falls to 1.0990.

It finally closes at 1.1030.

The candle is bullish because the close (1.1030) is above the open (1.1000).

The candle also tells us that price traded both above and below the opening level during the period.

11. Candles Are Not Guaranteed Signals

A common beginner mistake is believing that one candlestick automatically predicts what the market will do next.

Candlestick analysis becomes more useful when combined with broader context such as market structure, important price levels, volatility and risk management.

We will study these concepts in later lessons.

12. Basic Chart Reading Process

When opening a chart, you can begin with a simple process:

  • Identify the instrument.
  • Choose an appropriate timeframe.
  • Observe recent price movement.
  • Look at the relationship between highs and lows.
  • Identify important price areas.
  • Avoid making a trade simply because one candle looks interesting.

13. Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Looking at only one candle.
  • Ignoring the timeframe.
  • Assuming every large candle will continue in the same direction.
  • Entering trades without considering risk.
  • Using complicated indicators before understanding basic price movement.
