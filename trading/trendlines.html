TRADING ACADEMY • LESSON 07

Trendlines & Channels

Learn how traders use trendlines and channels to organize price movement and identify the direction and boundaries of a market.

1. What Is a Trendline?

A trendline is a line drawn on a price chart to help visualize the general direction of price movement.

Trendlines are normally drawn using significant swing points rather than random individual candles.

Important:

A trendline is an analytical tool. It is not a guarantee that price will continue in the same direction.

2. Upward Trendline

During an upward-moving market, traders may draw a trendline through important swing lows.

The line helps visualize whether successive pullbacks are generally forming at higher levels.

Illustration of an upward-sloping trendline

3. Downward Trendline

During a downward-moving market, traders may draw a trendline through important swing highs.

This can help visualize whether successive rallies are generally forming at lower levels.

Illustration of a downward-sloping trendline

4. How to Draw an Uptrend Line

  • Identify a clear upward structure.
  • Find meaningful swing lows.
  • Connect the relevant lows with a straight line.
  • Extend the line forward to observe future price behavior.
  • Avoid forcing the line through insignificant price movements.

5. How to Draw a Downtrend Line

  • Identify a clear downward structure.
  • Find meaningful swing highs.
  • Connect the relevant highs with a straight line.
  • Extend the line forward.
  • Observe how price behaves around the line.

6. How Many Touches Are Needed?

There is no universal rule that guarantees a trendline becomes valid after an exact number of touches.

Traders often look for multiple meaningful interactions with a trendline because repeated reactions can provide more context.

Don't chase perfection.

Markets are not perfectly geometric. A trendline is a visual approximation of price structure.

7. Trendline Breaks

A trendline break occurs when price moves through a trendline that traders have been watching.

A break may indicate that the previous pattern is changing, but the break itself does not guarantee a reversal.

Price can break a trendline and continue in the same general direction after a temporary move.

8. False Trendline Breaks

Price can temporarily move through a trendline and then return to the previous side.

This is sometimes called a false break.

Example

Imagine price has been making higher lows and respecting an upward trendline.

Price briefly moves below the line but then recovers and continues upward.

The temporary break alone would not prove that the entire trend has reversed.

9. Trendlines and Market Structure

Trendlines work best when combined with the structure concepts from Lesson 05.

For example, an upward trendline may support a market structure that is producing Higher Highs and Higher Lows.

A downward trendline may accompany a structure containing Lower Highs and Lower Lows.

Structure Possible Trendline
HH + HL Upward trendline through swing lows
LH + LL Downward trendline through swing highs
Mixed structure Trendline may be less reliable or unclear

10. What Is a Channel?

A channel consists of two roughly parallel lines that help describe the boundaries of price movement.

One line can represent the main trendline while the other provides a parallel boundary.

Illustration of a price channel

11. Ascending Channel

An ascending channel slopes upward.

Price may move between an upper boundary and a lower boundary while the overall structure moves higher.

The boundaries are areas of interest, not guaranteed turning points.

12. Descending Channel

A descending channel slopes downward.

Price may move between upper and lower boundaries while the overall structure moves lower.

13. Channels and Support/Resistance

Channels can be viewed as dynamic areas of potential support and resistance.

The lower boundary may act as an area where buyers have previously appeared, while the upper boundary may act as an area where sellers have previously appeared.

However, price can break through either boundary.

14. Trendline Confluence

A trendline becomes more interesting when it aligns with other forms of market information.

For example:

  • A significant support or resistance zone
  • A clear market-structure point
  • A higher-timeframe level
  • A previous swing high or swing low
Confluence:

Confluence means multiple independent pieces of analysis point toward the same area or scenario. It still does not remove trading risk.

15. Common Trendline Mistakes

  • Forcing a trendline to fit the chart.
  • Connecting insignificant price points.
  • Drawing dozens of trendlines.
  • Assuming every trendline touch is a trade signal.
  • Treating a trendline break as a guaranteed reversal.
  • Ignoring the higher timeframe.
  • Using trendlines without a risk-management plan.

16. A Simple Trendline Analysis Process

  • Start with a higher timeframe.
  • Identify the broader market structure.
  • Mark significant swing highs and lows.
  • Draw the simplest trendline that represents the structure.
  • Check whether price respects or breaks the line.
  • Compare the trendline with support and resistance.
  • Move to a lower timeframe only when necessary.
  • Never assume the line guarantees the next move.

17. Practical Exercise

Your Task

Open a demo chart for EUR/USD.

Choose the 4-hour timeframe.

Find two or more meaningful swing lows if the market structure is rising.

Draw an upward trendline through those areas.

Then observe how price behaves when it approaches the line.

Do not place a real-money trade simply because price touches the trendline.
