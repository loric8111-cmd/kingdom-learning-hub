TRADING ACADEMY • LESSON 05

Market Structure

Learn how traders describe the sequence of highs and lows to understand the current structure of a market.

1. What Is Market Structure?

Market structure describes how price forms successive highs and lows over time.

Instead of looking at individual candles, we step back and observe the larger sequence of price swings.

Key idea:

Market structure helps organize price action into a framework. It does not guarantee where price will move next.

2. The Four Basic Terms

Four abbreviations are especially important when learning basic market structure:

HH

Higher High

A high that forms above a previous significant high.

HL

Higher Low

A low that forms above a previous significant low.

LH

Lower High

A high that forms below a previous significant high.

LL

Lower Low

A low that forms below a previous significant low.

3. Higher High — HH

A Higher High occurs when price creates a new significant high above a previous high.

Example

Previous high: 1.1050

New high: 1.1100

The new high is higher than the previous high, so it can be described as a Higher High.

4. Higher Low — HL

A Higher Low occurs when price pulls back but forms a significant low above the previous significant low.

Example

Previous low: 1.0950

New pullback low: 1.1000

The new low is above the previous low. This can be described as a Higher Low.

5. Lower High — LH

A Lower High occurs when price rises but fails to reach the previous significant high.

Example

Previous high: 1.1100

New high: 1.1050

The new high is below the previous high. This can be described as a Lower High.

6. Lower Low — LL

A Lower Low occurs when price creates a new significant low below a previous significant low.

Example

Previous low: 1.1000

New low: 1.0950

The new low is below the previous low. This can be described as a Lower Low.

7. Basic Uptrend Structure

A simple upward market structure is commonly described as a sequence of Higher Highs and Higher Lows.

Basic Uptrend
HH → HL → HH → HL → HH

This sequence indicates that successive swing highs and lows are generally progressing upward.

8. Basic Downtrend Structure

A simple downward market structure is commonly described as a sequence of Lower Highs and Lower Lows.

Basic Downtrend
LL → LH → LL → LH → LL

This sequence indicates that successive swing highs and lows are generally progressing downward.

9. What Is a Range?

Markets do not always trend strongly upward or downward.

Sometimes price moves between relatively clear upper and lower boundaries without establishing a sustained sequence of higher highs and higher lows or lower highs and lower lows.

This type of environment is commonly called a range or consolidation.

Important:

A ranging market can behave differently from a trending market. Do not force every chart into an uptrend or downtrend.

10. Swing Highs and Swing Lows

Market structure is normally based on meaningful price swings rather than every tiny movement on a chart.

A swing high is an area where price turns after moving upward. A swing low is an area where price turns after moving downward.

The significance of a swing can depend on the timeframe and surrounding price action.

11. Multiple Timeframes

Market structure can be analyzed on different timeframes.

A market may show an upward structure on a short-term chart while showing a downward structure on a higher timeframe.

Example

A 15-minute chart may show:

HH → HL → HH

while a 4-hour chart could still be showing a broader downward structure.

This is why timeframe context matters.

12. Break of Structure

Traders sometimes use the term "break of structure" when price moves beyond a previously important swing point.

For example, if price has been forming a series of lower highs and lower lows and then moves above an important previous swing high, a trader may describe that movement as a break of structure.

The interpretation depends on the timeframe, the significance of the swing and the broader market context.

13. Structure Does Not Predict the Future

One of the most important lessons is that market structure is descriptive, not a guarantee.

A market can appear bullish and then reverse. A market can appear bearish and then recover. A breakout can fail.

Good analysis therefore needs to be combined with risk management and a clearly defined trading plan.

14. A Simple Structure-Reading Process

  • Choose the timeframe you want to analyze.
  • Identify the most meaningful recent swing highs.
  • Identify the most meaningful recent swing lows.
  • Compare the new highs with previous highs.
  • Compare the new lows with previous lows.
  • Look for HH, HL, LH and LL sequences.
  • Determine whether the market appears to be trending or ranging.
  • Do not make a trading decision from structure alone.

15. Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Marking every tiny price movement as a major swing.
  • Ignoring the timeframe.
  • Calling every price increase an uptrend.
  • Calling every price decrease a downtrend.
  • Assuming a break of structure guarantees a continuation.
  • Entering a trade simply because HH or LL appears.
  • Ignoring risk management.
