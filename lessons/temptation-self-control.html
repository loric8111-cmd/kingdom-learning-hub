BIBLE STUDIES • LESSON 07

Temptation & Self-Control

Learn how to recognize temptation, strengthen self-control and respond to difficult moments through faith, wisdom, prayer and Scripture.

1. What Is Temptation?

Temptation is an invitation or pressure to choose something that goes against God's will or against the values we are trying to live by.

Being tempted is not the same as choosing to sin. A person can experience temptation without giving in to it.

Remember:

Temptation may come, but you still have a choice about how you respond.

2. Where Can Temptation Lead?

Temptation can appeal to desires that are difficult to control. If those desires are repeatedly entertained and acted upon, they can become destructive habits.

Examples can include:

  • Sexual temptation and lust
  • Greed and love of money
  • Anger and desire for revenge
  • Pride
  • Dishonesty
  • Substance abuse
  • Envy and jealousy
  • Uncontrolled entertainment or other habits

3. Jesus Was Tempted

The Bible describes Jesus being tempted in the wilderness after fasting for forty days.

Matthew 4:1–11

Jesus responded to the devil's temptations by appealing to God's Word rather than surrendering to the temptation.

This account teaches an important lesson: knowing Scripture can help us recognize what is true and choose how to respond when we are tested.

4. Temptation Often Begins Before the Action

A destructive action can begin with what we allow our minds to repeatedly entertain.

For this reason, learning to recognize thoughts, situations and influences that repeatedly lead us toward temptation is important.

Example

Someone repeatedly exposes themselves to content that stimulates a desire they are trying to control.

Eventually the desire becomes stronger and the person finds it harder to resist.

A wiser response may be to identify the trigger early and remove themselves from it.

5. Self-Control

Self-control is the ability to govern your thoughts, words and actions rather than being controlled by every desire or emotion.

Galatians 5:22–23

Self-control is listed among the fruit associated with the work of the Holy Spirit.

Christian self-control is therefore not simply about human willpower. It is also about allowing God to shape our character and choices.

6. Recognize Your Triggers

A trigger is something that makes a particular temptation more likely to appear.

Triggers can include:

  • Certain environments
  • Specific websites or social media content
  • Certain friendships or influences
  • Loneliness
  • Boredom
  • Anger
  • Stress
  • Unhealthy routines

7. A Practical Response to Temptation

STEP 01

Recognize

Identify what is happening instead of pretending the temptation does not exist.

STEP 02

Pause

Create space between the temptation and your reaction.

STEP 03

Pray

Turn your attention toward God and ask for strength and wisdom.

STEP 04

Remove Yourself

If possible, leave the environment or situation that is encouraging the temptation.

STEP 05

Replace

Replace the destructive activity with something healthy and constructive.

STEP 06

Reflect

Afterward, consider what triggered the temptation and what you can change next time.

8. The Power of Prayer

Prayer gives us an opportunity to bring our struggles honestly before God and ask for strength, wisdom and guidance.

Matthew 26:41

Jesus taught His disciples to watch and pray so that they would not fall into temptation.

9. The Importance of God's Word

Scripture can help shape the way we think, identify what is right and remind us of God's truth when emotions become strong.

Psalm 119:11

The psalmist describes storing God's Word in the heart as a way of guarding against sin.

10. Choose Your Environment Wisely

Sometimes self-control becomes harder because we repeatedly place ourselves in environments that encourage the behavior we are trying to leave.

Wisdom may require changing routines, avoiding certain influences or creating healthier boundaries.

Wisdom is preventive.

You do not always have to prove that you can resist a temptation by deliberately staying close to it.

11. Choose Your Influences

The people, content and ideas around us can influence our attitudes and behavior.

Ask yourself:

  • What kind of content am I consuming?
  • Who influences my decisions?
  • Does my environment encourage the person I want to become?
  • What influences should I reduce?

12. What If You Fall?

Christian growth does not mean pretending that you will never make mistakes.

When someone falls into sin, the appropriate response is not to give up on God or decide that change is impossible.

Instead, acknowledge the wrong, turn back to God, seek forgiveness and learn from what happened.

1 John 1:9

The verse teaches that God is faithful and just to forgive and cleanse those who confess their sins.

Failure is not the end of the journey.

Use a fall as an opportunity to examine the trigger, strengthen your boundaries and continue growing.

13. Building Better Habits

Removing a destructive habit is often easier when you deliberately replace it with a constructive one.

For example:

  • Replace excessive idle time with learning.
  • Replace harmful content with useful content.
  • Replace isolation with healthy community.
  • Replace impulsive reactions with prayer and reflection.
  • Replace destructive routines with disciplined daily habits.

14. Daily Self-Control Practice

The Daily Reset

Each morning, identify one area where you want to practice greater self-control.

During the day, pay attention to the situations that test you.

At night, reflect honestly:

  • What temptation did I face?
  • What triggered it?
  • How did I respond?
  • What did I learn?
  • What can I do differently tomorrow?

15. Remember Your Purpose

When temptation becomes strong, remembering the person you want to become can help you make a better decision in the present moment.

Christian growth involves becoming more mature in faith, character, wisdom and love.

Ask yourself:

"Does this choice move me closer to the person God is calling me to become, or further away?"

16. Key Scriptures for Reflection

  • Matthew 4:1–11 — Jesus and temptation
  • Matthew 26:41 — Watch and pray
  • 1 Corinthians 10:13 — God provides a way of escape from temptation
  • Galatians 5:22–23 — Fruit of the Spirit
  • Psalm 119:11 — God's Word and resisting sin
  • James 1:12–15 — Understanding temptation
  • 1 John 1:9 — Confession and forgiveness
