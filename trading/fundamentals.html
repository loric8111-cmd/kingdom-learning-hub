TRADING ACADEMY • LESSON 01

Trading Fundamentals

Before learning strategies, indicators or advanced analysis, understand what trading actually is and how financial markets work.

1. What Is Trading?

Trading is the process of buying and selling financial instruments with the objective of potentially benefiting from changes in their prices.

Financial markets include instruments such as currencies, stocks, commodities, indices and other financial products.

Key idea:

Trading is not simply about predicting whether a price will go up or down. A trader must also consider risk, position size, timing, costs and the possibility of being wrong.

2. Trading vs Investing

Trading and investing both involve financial markets, but they can differ in time horizon and approach.

  • Trading: Often focuses on shorter-term price movements.
  • Investing: Often focuses on holding assets for longer periods based on long-term expectations.

Neither approach automatically guarantees profit. Both involve risk and require informed decision-making.

3. What Is a Financial Market?

A financial market is a system where participants buy and sell financial assets or contracts. Prices change as market participants place orders and react to information, expectations, supply and demand.

4. Major Markets

Traders can participate in different types of markets.

  • Forex: The market for exchanging currencies.
  • Stocks: Shares representing ownership in companies.
  • Commodities: Markets involving resources such as gold, oil and agricultural products.
  • Indices: Instruments representing the performance of a group of stocks.

5. Buyers and Sellers

Markets involve participants willing to buy and participants willing to sell.

When you enter a trade, your position exists within a market where other participants may have different expectations about price.

Simple Example

Imagine EUR/USD is trading at 1.1000. A trader believes the price may rise and opens a position. If the trader later closes the position at a higher price, the trade may produce a gain before costs.

However, if price moves in the opposite direction, the trade may produce a loss.

6. Long and Short Positions

Two basic concepts you'll encounter in trading are long and short positions.

  • Long: A trader generally benefits if the instrument's price rises after entering the position.
  • Short: A trader generally benefits if the instrument's price falls after entering the position.

The exact mechanics depend on the financial instrument and trading platform.

7. Risk Comes Before Profit

One of the most important principles in trading education is understanding risk before thinking about potential returns.

A trader can be correct about market direction and still lose money because of excessive position size, poor risk management, costs, leverage or an unfavorable price movement.

Remember:

There is no guaranteed trading strategy. Every trade carries uncertainty.

8. What Is Leverage?

Leverage allows a trader to control a position larger than the amount of capital deposited, subject to the broker's terms and margin requirements.

Leverage can increase exposure to both gains and losses. It does not make a trade safer.

9. Why Beginners Should Practice

Before risking real money, beginners can use demo accounts to learn how orders, charts, spreads, stop-losses and trading platforms work.

Demo practice does not completely reproduce the psychological experience of risking real money, but it can help develop technical familiarity and a basic process.

10. Your First Trading Principles

  • Never assume a trade is guaranteed to win.
  • Understand the instrument before trading it.
  • Learn risk management before increasing position size.
  • Keep records of your trades and decisions.
  • Avoid trading based purely on emotion.
  • Practice on a demo account while learning.
  • Do not risk money you cannot afford to lose.
