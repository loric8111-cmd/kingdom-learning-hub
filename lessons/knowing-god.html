BIBLE STUDIES • LESSON 02

Knowing God

Discover what it means to know God—not merely to know information about Him, but to develop a life-changing relationship with Him through faith, Scripture, prayer, obedience and fellowship.

1. What Does It Mean to Know God?

Knowing God is one of the deepest themes in Christian faith. It involves more than learning facts about God's existence, character or laws. It involves responding to God personally and developing a relationship with Him.

A person can know many facts about another person without actually having a relationship with them. In the same way, someone can know Bible information without allowing that knowledge to shape their relationship with God.

Key idea:

Knowing God involves both truth and relationship. We learn who God is through His revelation and respond to Him through faith, trust, worship, obedience and love.

2. Knowing About God vs. Knowing God

📚

Knowing About God

Learning facts about God's character, His works, His commands, His promises and the biblical story.

❤️

Knowing God

Living in a genuine relationship with God that affects your trust, decisions, character and life.

The two should not be separated. Biblical relationship with God is built upon truth about who God actually is.

3. God Makes Himself Known

Christianity begins with the conviction that God has revealed Himself. Human beings do not have to invent their own picture of God.

God reveals Himself through creation, Scripture and ultimately through Jesus Christ.

Romans 1:20

Paul explains that God's invisible qualities can be perceived through what He has made.

Hebrews 1:1–3

The passage presents Jesus Christ as the supreme revelation of God and describes Him as the exact representation of God's being.

4. Jesus Christ and Knowing God

Jesus occupies a central place in Christian understanding of knowing God.

According to the Gospel of John, eternal life is connected with knowing the only true God and Jesus Christ whom He sent.

John 17:3

Jesus describes eternal life in terms of knowing the true God and Jesus Christ whom God sent.

This shows that knowing God is not presented merely as information. It is connected with relationship, faith and eternal life.

5. Knowing God Begins With Faith

Faith is the response of trust toward God. Christian faith involves trusting God's character, believing His promises and responding to His revelation.

Hebrews 11:6

The writer teaches that faith is essential in approaching God and that those who come to Him must believe that He exists and rewards those who earnestly seek Him.

Faith does not mean pretending that questions do not exist. It means choosing to trust God while continuing to seek truth and understanding.

6. Knowing God's Character

The more we study Scripture, the more we discover what the Bible teaches about God's character.

❤️

God Is Love

God's love is central to the biblical story and is demonstrated supremely in His sending of His Son.

⚖️

God Is Just

God's justice means that He is not morally corrupt or indifferent to righteousness and wrongdoing.

🕊️

God Is Holy

God's holiness distinguishes Him from creation and points to His absolute moral purity.

🛡️

God Is Faithful

Scripture repeatedly presents God as trustworthy and faithful to His promises.

Exodus 34:6–7

God reveals Himself as merciful, gracious, patient and abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness, while also taking righteousness and wrongdoing seriously.

7. God's Names and What They Reveal

Throughout Scripture, names and descriptions of God communicate important truths about His character and relationship with His people.

  • Creator — God is the source of creation.
  • Shepherd — God guides and cares for His people.
  • Father — Scripture uses fatherhood to describe God's relationship with His people.
  • Lord — God has authority and rightful rule.
  • King — God is presented as sovereign.

8. Knowing God Through Scripture

The Bible is central to developing knowledge of God because it records God's revelation and tells the story of His relationship with humanity.

2 Timothy 3:16–17

Scripture is described as useful for teaching, correcting, training and equipping God's people for good works.

Bible study should therefore move beyond simply collecting information. Ask:

  • What does this passage reveal about God?
  • What does it reveal about humanity?
  • What does it teach about God's will?
  • How should this truth influence my life?

9. Knowing God Through Prayer

Prayer creates a pattern of communication, dependence, worship, confession, thanksgiving and seeking God's guidance.

Jeremiah 29:12–13

God promises that those who call upon Him and seek Him wholeheartedly will find Him.

Prayer should not be treated as a performance. It is an opportunity to approach God honestly, with reverence and trust.

10. Knowing God Through Obedience

A growing relationship with God should affect how a person lives.

John 14:15

Jesus connects love for Him with keeping His commandments.

Obedience does not mean earning God's love. Instead, obedience is a response to God and demonstrates that His teaching is being taken seriously.

11. Knowing God Through Love

Knowledge of God should transform the way we treat other people.

1 John 4:7–8

John teaches that love has its source in God and connects knowing God with loving others.

This gives us an important test: if our supposed knowledge of God never affects our treatment of other people, something is missing from our spiritual development.

12. Knowing God Through Christian Fellowship

Christian growth is not designed to happen entirely alone.

Fellowship provides opportunities for encouragement, teaching, accountability, worship and service. Other believers can help us see weaknesses that we might not recognize ourselves.

Hebrews 10:24–25

Believers are encouraged to consider how they can encourage one another toward love and good works and to continue meeting together.

13. Knowing God Is a Lifelong Pursuit

Knowing God is not something that ends after one prayer, one church service or one Bible lesson. It is a continuing journey.

Philippians 3:8–10

Paul expresses his desire to know Christ more deeply and to experience the transforming reality of belonging to Him.

This gives Christian growth a forward-looking character. There is always more to learn about God's character and more opportunity to become more faithful in following Him.

14. What Can Prevent Us From Knowing God?

Several attitudes can interfere with genuine knowledge of God.

  • Pride — believing we already know everything.
  • Hypocrisy — presenting an outward image while refusing inward change.
  • Neglect — refusing to make time for Scripture and prayer.
  • Disobedience — repeatedly ignoring what we understand God has commanded.
  • Distraction — allowing temporary things to consume our attention.
  • False ideas about God — creating a picture of God based only on personal preferences rather than Scripture.

15. Knowing God Changes the Way We Live

The purpose of knowing God is not simply to become better at religious discussion. Knowledge of God should influence our decisions, relationships, priorities and character.

Ask yourself:

"If I am truly growing in my knowledge of God, what evidence should appear in my everyday life?"

Greater knowledge should lead toward greater trust, humility, obedience, love, wisdom and dependence upon God.

16. A Practical Daily Pattern

📖

Read

Read a portion of Scripture slowly and carefully.

🔎

Understand

Ask what the passage actually teaches about God.

🙏

Pray

Respond to what you have learned through prayer.

🛠️

Apply

Identify one practical way to live out the lesson.

17. A Simple Example

Suppose you study a passage that teaches that God is patient.

Knowing the information is the first step. But deeper knowledge asks:

  • Do I trust God's patience toward me?
  • Am I becoming more patient with other people?
  • Do I remain patient when life does not move according to my plans?
  • Does God's character influence my character?

This is where biblical knowledge begins to move from information toward transformation.

18. Reflection: Do I Really Know God?

Take time to examine your spiritual life honestly. This is not about comparing yourself with another person. It is about examining the direction of your own relationship with God.

Questions for Reflection

  • What is the difference between knowing about God and knowing God?
  • What does Scripture reveal about God's character?
  • How does Jesus help us understand God?
  • How does Bible study help us know God?
  • How does prayer contribute to our relationship with God?
  • Why should knowing God affect our behavior?
  • How can Christian fellowship help spiritual growth?
  • What distractions make it difficult for you to seek God consistently?
  • Which area needs growth: Scripture, prayer, obedience, love or fellowship?
  • What practical step will you take this week to pursue God more intentionally?

19. Key Scriptures for This Lesson

  • John 17:3 — Knowing God and Jesus Christ
  • Jeremiah 29:12–13 — Seeking God wholeheartedly
  • Hebrews 11:6 — Faith and seeking God
  • Exodus 34:6–7 — God's revealed character
  • John 14:15 — Love and obedience
  • 1 John 4:7–8 — Knowing God and love
  • Philippians 3:8–10 — Knowing Christ
  • 2 Timothy 3:16–17 — The value of Scripture
  • Hebrews 10:24–25 — Christian fellowship
Final Thought

The goal of Bible study is not simply to fill your mind with information. The goal is to know God according to the truth He has revealed, trust Him, walk with Him and allow that relationship to transform the way you live.
