Currency Pairs

Learn how forex currencies are paired, how to read a currency quote and how price movement is interpreted in the foreign exchange market.

1. What Is a Currency Pair?

In forex, currencies are normally quoted as pairs because the value of one currency is expressed relative to another currency.

For example, EUR/USD compares the euro with the US dollar.

Remember:

The first currency is the base currency. The second currency is the quote currency.

2. Understanding EUR/USD

EUR/USD = 1.1000

EUR = Base currency

USD = Quote currency

The quotation means that one euro is valued at 1.1000 US dollars at that quoted exchange rate.

3. Base Currency

The base currency is the first currency written in a forex pair.

When you see:

GBP/USD

GBP is the base currency.

The price tells you how many units of the quote currency are needed to express one unit of the base currency.

4. Quote Currency

The quote currency is the second currency in the pair.

For USD/JPY, USD is the base currency and JPY is the quote currency.

USD/JPY = 150.00

A simplified interpretation is that one US dollar is being quoted at 150 Japanese yen.

5. Major Currency Pairs

Major pairs commonly involve the US dollar and another major global currency.

Pair Base Quote
EUR/USD EUR USD
GBP/USD GBP USD
USD/JPY USD JPY
USD/CHF USD CHF
AUD/USD AUD USD
USD/CAD USD CAD
NZD/USD NZD USD

6. Minor Currency Pairs

Minor pairs generally involve major currencies but do not include the US dollar.

Examples include:

  • EUR/GBP
  • EUR/JPY
  • GBP/JPY
  • AUD/JPY

Their liquidity and trading costs can differ from those of major pairs.

7. Exotic Currency Pairs

Exotic pairs generally combine a major currency with the currency of an emerging or smaller economy.

Examples can include USD/TRY and USD/ZAR.

Exotic pairs may have wider spreads and can experience significant volatility, depending on market conditions.

8. What Happens When a Pair Rises?

Suppose EUR/USD moves from:

1.1000 → 1.1050

The quoted value of the euro relative to the US dollar has increased.

A trader holding a long EUR/USD position would generally benefit from this price movement, before considering trading costs.

9. What Happens When a Pair Falls?

Suppose EUR/USD moves from:

1.1000 → 1.0950

The quoted value of the euro relative to the US dollar has decreased.

A trader holding a long EUR/USD position would generally experience a loss from that price movement, before costs.

10. Long and Short

A trader can take a position based on an expectation of rising or falling prices.

  • Long: You generally seek to benefit from an increase in the pair's price.
  • Short: You generally seek to benefit from a decrease in the pair's price.

Whether a trade is profitable depends on the entry price, exit price, position size, costs and other factors.

11. What Is a Pip?

A pip is a conventional unit used to describe small changes in many forex exchange rates.

For many non-JPY currency pairs, one pip is 0.0001. For many JPY pairs, one pip is 0.01.

EUR/USD Example

1.1000 → 1.1010

This is a movement of 10 pips under the four-decimal convention.

12. Spread

The spread is the difference between the bid and ask prices.

Example

Bid: 1.1000

Ask: 1.1002

Spread: 2 pips

Trading costs can affect the outcome of a trade, so traders should understand spreads and other fees before trading.

13. Currency Pair Selection

Beginners should avoid choosing a currency pair simply because it moves quickly.

Before trading a pair, consider:

  • Liquidity
  • Spread
  • Volatility
  • Trading session
  • Economic news
  • Your trading plan
Professional mindset:

The goal is not to trade as many currency pairs as possible. The goal is to understand the instruments you choose to trade.

14. Common Mistakes

  • Confusing the base and quote currencies.
  • Assuming a rising pair always means both currencies are becoming stronger.
  • Ignoring the spread.
  • Trading highly volatile pairs without understanding the risks.
  • Opening positions simply because a pair "looks cheap."
  • Using excessive leverage.
