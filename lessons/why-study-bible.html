BIBLE STUDY • CHAPTER 1

Why Study the Bible?

Discover why Scripture matters, how it reveals God, and how studying God's Word can influence faith, wisdom, character, and everyday life.

Introduction

The Bible is central to the Christian faith. It contains Scripture that teaches about God, humanity, faith, wisdom, salvation, and how believers are called to live.

Studying the Bible is more than simply reading words on a page. It involves seeking to understand the message of Scripture, examining its context, reflecting on its meaning, and considering how its teachings should influence our lives.

"All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting and training in righteousness."

2 Timothy 3:16

1. To Know God

Knowing God is more than simply knowing facts about Him. A person can know many things about God without truly seeking Him or developing a relationship with Him.

The Bible teaches us to know God's character, understand His works, listen to His Word, trust Him, and allow His truth to shape the way we live.

The Bible helps us learn about God's character, His works, His promises, and His relationship with humanity. Through Scripture, Christians seek to understand who God is, what He desires, and how we can live according to His will.

"And this is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent."

— John 17:3

1.1 Understand His Nature

The first step in knowing God is learning who He is. The Bible describes God as the Creator, holy, righteous, loving, merciful, faithful, wise, and just.

These characteristics help us understand that God is not simply a powerful being who created the universe. Scripture presents Him as having a character and as being concerned with His creation.

God Is Love

God's love is demonstrated through His concern for humanity and, most importantly, through Jesus Christ. His love is not merely an emotion; it is demonstrated through His actions and His willingness to provide a way for humanity to be reconciled to Him.

"God is love."

— 1 John 4:8

God Is Just and Righteous

God's love does not mean that He ignores wrongdoing. The Bible also presents God as just and righteous. His justice shows that good and evil matter to Him.

God Is Merciful

Mercy means that God shows compassion toward people who are in need of forgiveness and restoration. Throughout Scripture, we see examples of God giving people opportunities to turn away from wrongdoing and return to Him.

God Is Faithful

God's faithfulness means that He can be trusted. When God makes a promise, His character is trustworthy. Scripture repeatedly shows God remaining faithful even when human beings fail.

God Is Holy

God's holiness means that He is completely pure and separate from sin. Understanding God's holiness helps us recognize why our relationship with Him should involve humility, repentance, obedience, and a desire to live according to His ways.

1.2 Learn What God Has Done

Knowing someone's character becomes easier when we study their actions. The Bible therefore records what God has done throughout history.

Scripture tells the story of creation, humanity's fall into sin, God's dealings with Israel, the prophets, and ultimately the coming of Jesus Christ. These accounts help us understand God's character through His actions.

  • Power: God is presented as the Creator of heaven and earth.
  • Wisdom: God's understanding is greater than human understanding.
  • Patience: God repeatedly gives people opportunities to repent.
  • Mercy: God provides forgiveness and restoration.
  • Justice: God takes righteousness and wrongdoing seriously.
  • Faithfulness: God remains faithful to His promises.
  • Redemptive Purpose: Scripture presents God's plan of salvation through Jesus Christ.

Studying God's actions helps us build our understanding of Him from Scripture rather than relying only on personal opinions, assumptions, or what other people say about Him.

1.3 Listen to God Through His Word

The Bible is central to Christian faith because it communicates God's revelation to humanity. When we study Scripture, we are not simply reading an ancient collection of stories.

We are examining teachings, history, poetry, wisdom literature, prophecy, letters, and accounts concerning Jesus Christ.

Reading Scripture carefully helps us ask important questions:

What does this passage teach about God?

What does it teach about humanity?

What does it reveal about right and wrong?

What does it teach about faith and obedience?

How should this truth affect my life?

This turns Bible reading from simply completing chapters into learning, reflection, and application.

"Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path."

— Psalm 119:105

1.4 Know God Through Jesus Christ

For Christians, Jesus Christ is central to understanding God. The New Testament presents Jesus as God's Son and teaches that His life, teachings, death, and resurrection are essential to God's plan of salvation.

Jesus taught people about God's character and demonstrated compassion, forgiveness, humility, truth, and obedience. Studying His life therefore helps Christians understand His teachings and how He calls His followers to live.

"Whoever has seen me has seen the Father."

— John 14:9

The four Gospels — Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John — are especially useful for someone beginning a journey of learning about Jesus Christ and His teachings.

1.5 Build a Relationship With God

Knowing God should not remain purely intellectual. There is a difference between knowing facts about someone and actually developing a relationship with that person.

For example, you can know someone's biography, occupation, family history, and personality without ever communicating with them. In the same way, someone can know many Bible verses without allowing that knowledge to become a genuine pursuit of God.

A growing relationship with God involves:

  • Prayer: Speaking honestly with God.
  • Bible Study: Listening to and learning from Scripture.
  • Faith: Trusting God even when circumstances are difficult.
  • Obedience: Putting God's teachings into practice.
  • Repentance: Recognizing wrongdoing and turning away from it.
  • Worship: Honoring God for who He is.
  • Love: Demonstrating God's principles toward other people.
  • Perseverance: Continuing to seek God during difficult seasons.

1.6 Knowing God Changes How We Live

The purpose of Bible study is not merely to become knowledgeable. True understanding should influence our character and the way we treat other people.

If we learn that God values forgiveness, we should become more willing to forgive. If we learn that God values honesty, we should become more truthful. If we learn to love others, that teaching should affect our relationships.

Knowing God should therefore affect our thoughts, words, decisions, relationships, priorities, and behavior.

"But be doers of the word, and not hearers only."

— James 1:22

1.7 Ask Questions While Studying

Bible study becomes more meaningful when you learn to investigate rather than simply read. For every passage, consider using the following three steps.

Observation — What does the passage say?

Look carefully at the people, events, commands, promises, warnings, repeated words, and major ideas contained in the passage.

Interpretation — What does the passage mean?

Consider the historical context, the surrounding verses, the purpose of the passage, and how it fits within the broader message of Scripture.

Application — What should I do with what I have learned?

Ask how the teaching should influence your thoughts, decisions, relationships, behavior, and relationship with God.

Read → Understand → Reflect → Apply → Grow

1.8 Know God Through His Creation

The Bible also points people toward God's creation. The beauty, order, complexity, and vastness of the natural world can lead people to reflect on the greatness of the Creator.

"The heavens declare the glory of God, and the sky above proclaims his handiwork."

— Psalm 19:1

Creation, however, should not replace Scripture. Rather, it can encourage us to recognize God's greatness, while Scripture provides deeper revelation concerning His character, purposes, and relationship with humanity.

1.9 Knowing God Requires Humility

One of the most important attitudes in Bible study is humility. Human understanding is limited, and we will encounter difficult passages and questions that require deeper study.

Sincere Christians may also disagree about the interpretation of certain subjects. Instead of pretending to understand everything, a student of Scripture should be willing to say:

"I don't understand this yet, but I am willing to learn."

This attitude creates room for learning, correction, wisdom, and spiritual growth.

"For the LORD gives wisdom; from his mouth come knowledge and understanding."

— Proverbs 2:6

1.10 The Goal: A Transformed Life

Ultimately, the purpose of knowing God is transformation. The more we understand God's character and teachings, the more we should examine our own character and the direction of our life.

Ask yourself:

  • Am I becoming more loving?
  • Am I becoming more honest?
  • Am I becoming more patient?
  • Am I learning self-control?
  • Am I becoming more forgiving?
  • Am I becoming more humble?
  • Am I treating other people with dignity?
  • Am I becoming more obedient to God's teachings?
  • Am I trusting God more than I did before?

Christian growth is therefore not simply about knowing more Bible verses. It is about allowing God's truth to shape the mind, heart, character, decisions, relationships, and direction of life.

A Simple Foundation for Knowing God

The journey of knowing God can be understood as a continuous process of learning and transformation:

Know God → Understand His Word → Believe His Truth → Apply His Teachings → Grow in Character → Serve Others → Continue Learning

Knowing God is not a lesson that ends after completing one Bible chapter or course. It is a lifelong journey of learning, reflecting, practicing, falling short, repenting, growing, and continually seeking to draw closer to God.

2. To Grow and Live Wisely

Knowing God should naturally lead us toward a wiser way of living. The Bible does not present wisdom as merely possessing a large amount of information. Biblical wisdom involves learning to recognize what is good, understanding the consequences of our choices, seeking God's direction, and putting truth into practice.

A wise person is not someone who knows everything. A wise person recognizes the limits of human understanding and is willing to listen, learn, receive correction, think carefully, and make decisions according to truth rather than impulse.

The book of Proverbs repeatedly connects wisdom with instruction, discipline, humility, understanding, self-control, righteousness, and the fear of the Lord. Scripture therefore presents wisdom as something that should shape our character, decisions, relationships, speech, work, finances, priorities, and response to difficult circumstances.

"If any of you lacks wisdom, you should ask God, who gives generously to all without finding fault, and it will be given to you."

James 1:5

2.1 Wisdom Begins With Reverence for God

Biblical wisdom begins with recognizing that God is the highest source of truth and that human beings do not know everything. This attitude is often described in Scripture as the fear of the Lord.

The fear of God does not simply mean being terrified of Him. It can include reverence, respect, humility, and recognition of God's authority. When a person takes God seriously, that person becomes more willing to listen to His instruction rather than assuming that personal desires are always right.

"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom."

— Proverbs 9:10

This gives us an important principle: true wisdom begins with humility. Before asking, "What do I want?" we should also learn to ask, "What is right?" and "What does God teach?"

2.2 Gain Daily Guidance

Life is filled with decisions. Some decisions appear small, while others can influence our future for many years. We make choices about friendships, relationships, work, money, education, speech, entertainment, habits, responsibilities, and how we respond to pressure.

The Bible provides principles that can help us think through these decisions. It teaches values such as honesty, diligence, self-control, patience, humility, justice, compassion, faithfulness, and love.

Scripture does not necessarily provide a specific verse telling us exactly what to do in every modern situation. Instead, it helps develop the moral framework and wisdom by which we evaluate our choices.

"Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path."

— Psalm 119:105

A lamp does not necessarily show a person the entire road at once. It provides enough light to take the next step. In the same way, Scripture can help us develop the wisdom to make the next faithful and responsible decision.

2.3 Wisdom Means Thinking Before Acting

One of the practical marks of wisdom is the ability to pause before reacting. Anger, fear, excitement, pride, jealousy, pressure, and desire can influence our decisions if we act without thinking.

Biblical wisdom encourages patience and self-control. Instead of immediately saying everything we think or doing whatever we feel, we learn to consider the consequences of our actions.

"Everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak and slow to become angry."

— James 1:19

This principle can transform everyday life. Before making a decision, we can ask:

  • Is it true? Am I acting on facts or assumptions?
  • Is it right? Does this choice agree with God's principles?
  • What are the consequences? How could this decision affect me and others?
  • What is motivating me? Am I being driven by wisdom, fear, pride, anger, pressure, or selfish desire?
  • Would I be comfortable bringing this decision before God? Does this choice reflect the character I want to develop?

2.4 Wisdom Includes Receiving Correction

A person cannot grow in wisdom while refusing every correction. One of the differences between wisdom and pride is the willingness to learn when we discover that we are wrong.

Correction can sometimes feel uncomfortable because it challenges our ego. However, appropriate correction can reveal weaknesses that we could not see by ourselves.

"Whoever heeds life-giving correction will be at home among the wise."

— Proverbs 15:31

Growing wisely therefore requires an attitude of teachability. Instead of asking only, "Who criticized me?" we should sometimes ask, "Is there something useful I can learn from this correction?"

2.5 Spot Error and Sin

The Bible also helps us recognize what is inconsistent with God's standards. Without a reliable standard, people can easily judge right and wrong according to personal feelings, culture, peer pressure, or changing opinions.

Scripture provides principles by which teachings, attitudes, actions, and motives can be examined. It can expose areas of our lives that require repentance, correction, maturity, and change.

"All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting and training in righteousness."

2 Timothy 3:16

Notice the progression: Scripture can teach us what is true, rebuke what is wrong, correct what needs to change, and train us to live more righteously.

This means Bible study should sometimes challenge us. If every passage simply confirms everything we already believe, we may not be allowing Scripture to examine us deeply enough.

2.6 Wisdom Helps Us Recognize False or Harmful Teaching

Growing in wisdom also means learning not to believe every statement simply because someone presents it confidently. People can misunderstand Scripture, manipulate verses, spread false teaching, or mix biblical ideas with personal opinions.

A growing student of Scripture learns to examine claims carefully. Instead of accepting every spiritual statement immediately, we can compare it with the wider teaching of Scripture and consider its context.

"Test everything; hold fast what is good."

— 1 Thessalonians 5:21

Discernment does not mean becoming suspicious of everyone. It means becoming careful about what we accept as truth. Wisdom learns to listen without becoming gullible.

2.7 Wisdom Changes the Way We Speak

Wisdom is visible not only in what we believe but also in how we speak. Words can encourage, heal, teach, reconcile, or cause unnecessary damage.

A wise person learns when to speak, when to remain silent, how to correct someone respectfully, and how to communicate truth without deliberately humiliating others.

"The words of the reckless pierce like swords, but the tongue of the wise brings healing."

— Proverbs 12:18

Before speaking, wisdom encourages us to consider whether our words are true, necessary, constructive, and appropriate.

2.8 Wisdom Helps Us Choose Our Influences

The people we spend time with can influence our thinking, habits, attitudes, ambitions, and decisions. This does not mean that Christians should treat other people with disrespect or isolate themselves from society. Rather, Scripture teaches the importance of recognizing the influence of our closest relationships.

"Walk with the wise and become wise, for a companion of fools suffers harm."

— Proverbs 13:20

A wise person therefore asks whether their friendships and environments are helping them become more disciplined, truthful, responsible, peaceful, and spiritually mature—or whether they are constantly pulling them toward destructive choices.

2.9 Wisdom Includes Self-Control

Wisdom is not only knowing what is right. It also involves having the discipline to act according to what we know. A person may understand that something is harmful and still continue doing it because desire is stronger than discipline.

Biblical wisdom therefore connects understanding with self-control. It teaches us to govern our words, reactions, desires, habits, and decisions rather than allowing every impulse to control us.

This is one reason spiritual growth requires practice. Knowledge becomes wisdom when truth begins to influence behavior.

Know the truth → Think carefully → Exercise self-control → Make wise choices → Build good habits

2.10 Wisdom in Difficult Times

Wisdom becomes especially important when life does not go according to our plans. Difficult seasons can produce fear, confusion, frustration, disappointment, and uncertainty.

Scripture encourages believers to seek God's wisdom rather than allowing difficult circumstances to completely control their decisions.

"Trust in the LORD with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways submit to him, and he will make your paths straight."

— Proverbs 3:5–6

Trusting God does not mean refusing to think or plan. It means recognizing that our understanding is limited and that God's wisdom is greater than our own. We can plan carefully while remaining humble enough to accept that circumstances may change.

2.11 Find Comfort, Hope, and Encouragement

The Bible is not only a book of commands and correction. It also contains words of comfort, hope, encouragement, and reminders of God's faithfulness.

People experience grief, disappointment, loneliness, failure, uncertainty, rejection, financial pressure, conflict, and many other difficult circumstances. During such seasons, Scripture can remind believers that suffering is not the whole story.

"Why, my soul, are you downcast? Why so disturbed within me? Put your hope in God."

— Psalm 42:11

Biblical hope is not simply pretending that everything is fine. It is learning to place trust in God even when circumstances remain difficult.

This gives Bible study another purpose: Scripture can help shape our response to hardship. Instead of allowing fear or despair to become our only voice, we can return to God's promises, remember His character, pray, and continue moving forward with faith and wisdom.

2.12 Wisdom Is Practiced, Not Merely Collected

A person can memorize hundreds of Bible verses and still make unwise decisions. Biblical wisdom becomes meaningful when knowledge is translated into action.

For example, knowing that Scripture teaches forgiveness is knowledge. Choosing to forgive someone who has wronged you is an application of that knowledge.

Knowing that Scripture teaches honesty is knowledge. Refusing to lie when lying would appear easier or more profitable is wisdom in practice.

Knowing that Scripture teaches self-control is knowledge. Practicing restraint when your emotions or desires demand immediate satisfaction is wisdom in action.

"But be doers of the word, and not hearers only."

— James 1:22

2.13 The Difference Between Knowledge and Wisdom

It is helpful to understand the difference between these ideas:

  • Knowledge: Knowing information or understanding facts.
  • Understanding: Grasping the meaning and significance of what you have learned.
  • Wisdom: Knowing how to apply truth correctly in real situations.
  • Discernment: Recognizing what is true, false, beneficial, harmful, wise, or foolish.
  • Character: Allowing repeated wise choices to shape who you become.
Knowledge → Understanding → Discernment → Wise Action → Character → Mature Living

2.14 A Practical Wisdom Check

Before making an important decision, pause and examine it carefully. Ask yourself:

What does Scripture teach about this kind of situation?

Am I being honest with myself about my motives?

Could this decision harm me or someone else?

Am I acting from wisdom or from temporary emotion?

Have I considered the long-term consequences?

Am I willing to receive wise counsel?

Have I prayed and genuinely sought God's wisdom?

Would this decision help me become the person I want to become?

2.15 The Goal: A Wise and Steady Life

The goal of biblical wisdom is not to make us appear intelligent in front of other people. It is to develop a life characterized by truth, humility, discipline, righteousness, love, courage, patience, responsibility, and faith.

Wisdom teaches us to slow down when emotions are intense, listen before speaking, learn before judging, seek counsel before making major decisions, recognize our weaknesses, and remain willing to change when truth exposes something wrong in us.

A wise life is not a perfect life. Everyone makes mistakes. The difference is that wisdom teaches us to learn from mistakes, accept correction, repent when necessary, and continue growing.

A Simple Foundation for Growing in Wisdom

Biblical wisdom can be understood as a lifelong process of learning God's truth and allowing that truth to influence the way we think and live:

Seek God → Learn Truth → Gain Understanding → Test What You Hear → Make Wise Choices → Receive Correction → Practice Self-Control → Grow in Character → Live Wisely

To grow wisely is to become increasingly willing to listen, learn, think carefully, recognize error, receive correction, make responsible decisions, and trust God's wisdom above our own limited understanding.

The goal is not simply to become someone who knows more. The goal is to become someone who lives better because of what they have learned.

3. To Strengthen Faith

Regular engagement with Scripture can help believers reflect on God's promises and develop a deeper understanding of faith. Reading about God's faithfulness throughout Scripture can encourage believers to trust Him during difficult circumstances.

"So faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ."

Romans 10:17

4. To Guide Our Lives

The Bible provides principles that can help people think about their choices, relationships, character, responsibilities, and direction in life.

"Your word is a lamp for my feet, a light on my path."

Psalm 119:105

How Should We Study the Bible?

A useful approach is to slow down, read the passage carefully, consider its context, identify what it teaches, and reflect on how the message should influence our lives.

  • Read the passage carefully.
  • Consider the context.
  • Identify the main message.
  • Compare related Scripture where appropriate.
  • Reflect on how the lesson applies to life.
  • Pray for wisdom and understanding.

Reflection Questions

  • Why do you want to study the Bible?
  • What do you hope to learn about God?
  • Which area of your life needs greater wisdom and guidance?
  • What decision in your life currently requires wisdom?
  • Are you willing to receive correction when Scripture challenges you?
  • What influences are helping or hurting your growth in wisdom?
  • What is one wise biblical principle you can begin practicing today?
  • What can you do to make Bible study a consistent part of your life?
