Bible Studies • Lesson 02

Knowing God

What does it really mean to know God? Explore the difference between religious information and a genuine relationship with God.

Introduction: Knowing God Is More Than Religion

One of the greatest questions a Christian can ask is:

“Do I actually know God, or do I only know things about God?”

A person can attend church for years, listen to sermons, memorize Scripture, know Christian terminology, and still have very little personal fellowship with God.

The Bible presents knowing God as something much deeper.

It involves knowledge, relationship, trust, obedience, love, worship, transformation and perseverance.

Think about a student who knows everything about a famous teacher. The student may know the teacher's name, education, achievements, favorite subjects and teaching methods. But if the teacher has never personally interacted with the student, that student doesn't actually know the teacher in a relational sense.

The same distinction applies spiritually.

You can know the stories of Noah, Abraham, Moses and David; the Ten Commandments; the teachings of Jesus; biblical prophecy; Christian theology; and dozens of Bible verses—and still fail to surrender your life to God.

The central question is not simply:

“How much Bible knowledge do I have?”

It is:

“What is my knowledge of God doing inside my life?”

If knowing God is real, it should gradually influence the way you think, speak, make decisions, handle money, treat people, respond to temptation, deal with failure and face uncertainty.

1. What Does It Mean to Know God?

Knowing God means entering into a genuine relationship with Him based on the truth He has revealed about Himself.

It begins with discovering who God is, but it does not end there.

There is a difference between:

Information About God

Learning what Scripture teaches about God's character, His actions, His promises and His commands.

Relationship With God

Personally trusting God, responding to Him, walking with Him and allowing His truth to influence your life.

For example, you might know that God is faithful.

“Do I trust His faithfulness when my circumstances are difficult?”

You may know that God commands forgiveness.

“What do I do when somebody genuinely hurts me?”

You may know that God tells you not to be controlled by greed.

“How do I behave when I desperately need money?”

That is where theology becomes practical.

Real-World Example

Imagine a young person who has very little money and sees an opportunity to make quick money through something dishonest.

Knowing about God might tell him:

“The Bible says stealing is wrong.”

Knowing God should take him further:

“God sees what I am about to do. I trust Him enough to refuse dishonest gain, even though I need money.”

The second response demonstrates that biblical knowledge is beginning to influence behavior.

Key Principle

Knowing God means allowing the truth about God to shape the way you live before God.

2. Knowing About God vs. Knowing God

Knowing About God

This involves learning information:

  • God's attributes
  • His promises
  • His commandments
  • His actions throughout history
  • Biblical theology
  • The life and teachings of Jesus
  • Christian doctrine

This is important.

You cannot have a healthy relationship with God while believing false things about Him.

But information alone is insufficient.

Knowing God

Knowing God involves personal trust and fellowship with Him.

It affects:

  • Your priorities
  • Your conscience
  • Your decisions
  • Your character
  • Your relationships
  • Your response to temptation
  • Your understanding of suffering
  • Your attitude toward possessions
  • Your treatment of people
Modern Example

Consider someone who watches Christian videos every day but spends hours consuming content that continually feeds lust, hatred, envy or greed.

They may have accumulated enormous amounts of Christian information.

But the question remains:

“Is that knowledge transforming them?”

Christian maturity isn't measured simply by how much religious content someone consumes.

It is also measured by whether truth is producing spiritual fruit.

3. God Makes Himself Known

Christianity does not begin with humanity inventing a god.

It begins with God revealing Himself.

This is called divine revelation.

God makes Himself known through several means, including:

Creation

Creation points beyond itself to a Creator.

Romans 1:20

Romans 1:20 teaches that God's invisible qualities can be perceived through what He has made.

The universe raises fundamental questions:

  • Why does anything exist?
  • Why does the universe have order?
  • Why do humans possess moral awareness?
  • Why do we experience beauty?
  • Why do we search for meaning?

Creation does not provide every detail of Christian theology, but it points toward God's existence and power.

Scripture

The Bible provides God's revealed testimony concerning Himself, humanity, sin, salvation and His purposes.

Jesus Christ

Hebrews 1:1–3

Hebrews 1:1–3 presents Christ as the supreme revelation of God.

Jesus does not merely speak about God.

Christian faith teaches that in Jesus, God has made Himself known in a unique and decisive way.

Real-World Example

Today, people encounter thousands of competing ideas about God through TikTok, YouTube, podcasts, social media, artificial intelligence, influencers and religious debates.

The danger is allowing algorithms to become your theology.

A viral video can receive millions of views and still present a completely distorted picture of God.

Therefore, Christians must ask:

“Where did this idea about God come from?”

Popularity is not the same thing as truth.

4. Jesus Christ and Knowing God

Jesus is central to Christian understanding of God.

John 17:3

“And this is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.”

Notice that Jesus describes eternal life in terms of knowing God.

This does not mean that eternal life is merely intellectual knowledge.

The biblical idea involves relationship, recognition, trust and allegiance.

Jesus also said in John 14:6 that He is the way to the Father.

Therefore, Christianity does not treat Jesus as merely a moral teacher among many.

He is central to understanding who God is and how humanity is reconciled to God.

Real-World Example

Suppose someone says:

“I believe in God, but I don't need Jesus.”

Christian theology must take that statement seriously because Jesus Himself placed His identity and mission at the center of knowing God.

The Christian question isn't merely:

“Do you believe there is a God?”

It is:

“Who do you believe Jesus Christ is, and what does your response to Him mean for your life?”

5. Knowing God Begins With Faith

Hebrews 11:6

Hebrews 11:6 teaches that faith is essential in approaching God.

Faith is not simply believing something without thinking.

Biblical faith includes trust, reliance and commitment.

You can have questions and still have faith.

You can experience uncertainty and still seek God.

“I don't understand everything, but I choose to trust God while I continue seeking truth.”

Real-World Example

Imagine someone who has prayed for employment for months but remains unemployed.

Faith does not necessarily mean saying:

“Tomorrow I will definitely receive a job.”

That would be making a prediction.

Faith means:

“I will continue seeking God, working honestly, developing my skills and refusing to abandon my trust in Him simply because circumstances are difficult.”

Faith becomes visible through perseverance.

6. Knowing God's Character

You cannot truly know God while ignoring His character.

Exodus 34:6–7

God reveals Himself as merciful, gracious, patient, abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness, while also taking righteousness and wrongdoing seriously.

This gives us a balanced picture.

God is not merely “love without justice.”

Nor is He merely “judgment without mercy.”

Biblical theology holds together God's holiness, justice, mercy, love and faithfulness.

God Is Love

1 John 4:8

1 John 4:8 teaches that God is love.

God's love is not simply emotional approval.

Biblical love seeks what is genuinely good.

God Is Just

God does not treat evil as though it does not matter.

This matters when we experience injustice.

If someone cheats you, abuses authority or deliberately harms another person, Christianity does not require pretending that wrongdoing is acceptable.

God Is Holy

God's holiness means that He is completely distinct from sin and morally pure.

Knowing God's holiness should produce reverence rather than casualness toward God.

God Is Faithful

God's faithfulness means that He is trustworthy.

Real-World Example

Suppose your business is struggling.

You have two choices:

Option A: manipulate customers because you're desperate.

Option B: remain honest while looking for legitimate solutions.

Knowing God's faithfulness does not guarantee that your business will immediately succeed.

But it gives you a foundation for refusing to sacrifice your integrity simply to survive.

7. God's Names and What They Reveal

Biblical names and descriptions of God reveal aspects of His relationship with creation and humanity.

Creator

God is the source of creation.

This challenges the idea that human beings are ultimately self-made.

Shepherd

A shepherd guides, protects and cares for sheep. Psalm 23 uses this imagery to describe God's care.

Father

Scripture uses fatherhood to communicate relationship, care and authority.

Lord

God is not merely an adviser.

Lordship means rightful authority.

King

God's kingship communicates sovereignty.

Why This Matters Today

Modern culture strongly emphasizes:

“You are your own person. Do whatever you want.”

Christianity introduces another question:

“Who has the right to rule my life?”

If God is Lord, then your career, relationships, money, body, ambitions and future cannot be treated as completely independent from Him.

8. Knowing God Through Scripture

Scripture is essential because Christians need a reliable standard by which to evaluate their ideas about God.

2 Timothy 3:16–17

2 Timothy 3:16–17 describes Scripture as useful for teaching, correction, reproof, training and equipping God's people.

But Bible study can also become mechanical.

Someone can read ten chapters and remember almost nothing.

A deeper approach asks:

Observation

What does the passage actually say?

Interpretation

What did it mean in its context?

Theology

What does it teach about God, humanity, sin, salvation or God's purposes?

Application

How should this truth affect my life?

Transformation

What needs to change because of what I have learned?

Real-World Example

If you study Proverbs and learn about wisdom with money, don't stop at:

“Proverbs talks about money.”

Ask:

“Am I financially disciplined?”

“Do I spend money impulsively?”

“Do I borrow unnecessarily?”

“Am I honest in business?”

“Am I allowing money to control my decisions?”

Now Bible study is moving from information to formation.

9. Knowing God Through Prayer

Prayer is more than requesting things.

It includes:

  • Worship
  • Confession
  • Thanksgiving
  • Dependence
  • Asking
  • Listening through reflection on God's revealed Word
  • Seeking wisdom
  • Expressing sorrow
  • Expressing gratitude
Jeremiah 29:12–13

Jeremiah 29:12–13 speaks of seeking God wholeheartedly.

Prayer exposes our dependence.

Real-World Example

Imagine receiving a difficult message from your employer.

Your immediate reaction may be anger.

Instead of responding impulsively, you stop and pray:

“God, give me wisdom. Help me control my words. Help me understand what I should do.”

Prayer doesn't mean refusing to act.

It means bringing your actions under God's authority before acting.

10. Knowing God Through Obedience

John 14:15

John 14:15 connects love for Jesus with obedience.

This is extremely important.

Obedience is not a payment we give God in exchange for salvation.

Rather, obedience demonstrates that God's authority matters to us.

Example

Suppose someone says:

“I love God.”

But they repeatedly justify lying whenever lying benefits them.

Their words and behavior contradict each other.

Another person may struggle, fail and repent, but genuinely desire to become truthful.

The second person's life may demonstrate a deeper process of transformation.

Christian obedience does not mean never falling.

It means refusing to make peace with sin.

When you fall, you return to God, repent, learn and continue walking.

11. Knowing God Through Love

1 John 4:7–8

1 John 4:7–8 connects knowing God with loving others.

This is one of the strongest tests of spiritual maturity.

If your Christianity makes you arrogant, cruel, constantly hateful, unforgiving, dishonest or exploitative, something has gone seriously wrong.

Real-World Example

Imagine someone insulting you publicly online.

You have several possible responses:

  • Revenge
  • Humiliation
  • Insults
  • Threats
  • Spreading their private information
  • Calm correction
  • Silence
  • Forgiveness where appropriate

Knowing God should increasingly affect how you respond.

Love does not mean allowing abuse or refusing appropriate boundaries.

It means refusing to become evil simply because someone else behaved badly.

12. Knowing God Through Christian Fellowship

Christianity was not designed as a completely isolated journey.

Hebrews 10:24–25

Hebrews 10:24–25 encourages believers to encourage one another toward love and good works.

Healthy fellowship provides:

  • Encouragement
  • Correction
  • Teaching
  • Accountability
  • Prayer
  • Service
  • Shared worship
Modern Example

Social media can create the illusion of Christian community.

You might follow 500 Christian accounts but have nobody who actually knows your struggles, challenges your hypocrisy or encourages you when you're failing.

Digital content can supplement fellowship.

It cannot automatically replace meaningful human relationships.

A mature believer needs people who can say:

“Brother, I think you're going in the wrong direction.”

And rather than becoming defensive, the believer learns humility.

13. Knowing God Is a Lifelong Pursuit

Philippians 3:8–10

Philippians 3:8–10 shows Paul's continuing desire to know Christ.

Paul was already an experienced believer.

Yet he still wanted to know Christ more deeply.

This destroys the idea that spiritual maturity means:

“I've learned everything.”

You never reach a point where you can stop growing.

Think of it like this:

  • More character to develop
  • More wisdom to acquire
  • More selfishness to surrender
  • More love to demonstrate
  • More Scripture to understand
  • More faith to exercise

Christian maturity is not reaching perfection overnight.

It is moving continually toward Christ.

14. What Can Prevent Us From Knowing God?

Pride

“I already know enough.” Pride closes the mind.

Hypocrisy

Knowing the right words while refusing inward transformation.

Neglect

You cannot expect a relationship to deepen when you consistently refuse to invest time in it.

Disobedience

Repeatedly knowing what is right and deliberately refusing it hardens the heart.

Distraction

Modern life provides endless distractions.

“I don't have time to pray.”

The problem may not be the absence of time.

It may be the allocation of attention.

False Ideas About God

This is particularly dangerous.

People can create a god according to their preferences:

“My god would never judge.”

or:

“My god only cares about rules.”

Neither statement should be accepted simply because it sounds convincing.

We must ask:

“What has God actually revealed about Himself?”

15. Knowing God Changes the Way We Live

The ultimate test isn't how impressive your religious vocabulary sounds.

It is what happens in ordinary life.

Knowing God should progressively affect:

Money

Do I become dishonest when I need money?

Relationships

Do I use people or genuinely care about them?

Sexuality

Do I allow desire to control my decisions?

Anger

How do I respond when disrespected?

Work

Do I work honestly when nobody is watching?

Ambition

Do I want success merely to prove myself superior to others?

Failure

Does failure destroy my identity?

Success

Does success make me arrogant?

Suffering

Do difficult circumstances cause me to abandon every conviction I have?

Technology

Does technology serve me, or has it become the master of my attention?

This is where knowing God becomes visible.

The relationship becomes visible through ordinary decisions, especially when nobody is watching.

16. A Practical Daily Pattern

Use this simple pattern when studying Scripture.

Read

Read a manageable section carefully. Don't rush.

Understand

Ask:

  • What is actually happening?
  • Who is speaking?
  • Who is being addressed?
  • What is the context?

Discover

Ask:

“What does this reveal about God?”

Respond

Pray about what you discovered.

Apply

Choose one concrete action.

For example:

“Today I will apologize to the person I wronged.”

“Today I will refuse to lie about my work.”

“Today I will spend 20 minutes studying Scripture instead of scrolling.”

Review

At the end of the day ask:

“Did I actually practice what I learned?”

That final question is powerful.

17. A Real-World Example: God's Patience

From Information to Transformation

Suppose your Bible study teaches that God is patient.

You understand the doctrine.

But now imagine someone repeatedly annoys you. You become angry.

This creates a practical test.

Information: “I know God is patient.”

Understanding: “God demonstrates patience.”

Application: “I should practice patience.”

Transformation: “I am becoming slower to anger and more controlled in difficult situations.”

The goal is not merely knowing:

“God is patient.”

The goal is becoming a person whose character increasingly reflects what you have learned about God.

This is part of spiritual formation.

18. Reflection: Do I Really Know God?

This question requires honesty.

Don't ask:

“Do people think I'm a good Christian?”

Ask:

“What is actually happening between me and God?”

Consider your private life.

When nobody is watching:

  • What do you desire?
  • What do you worship?
  • What controls your attention?
  • What do you fear most?
  • What do you trust?
  • How do you respond to temptation?
  • How do you respond when God seems silent?
  • What happens when you receive money?
  • What happens when you lose money?
  • How do you treat people who cannot benefit you?
  • Do you seek God only when you need something?

Reflection Questions

  • What is the difference between knowing about God and knowing God?
  • What does God's revelation teach us about Him?
  • How does Jesus Christ help us understand God?
  • How does faith contribute to knowing God?
  • How can Scripture move from information to transformation?
  • How does prayer reveal our dependence upon God?
  • How does obedience demonstrate that God's authority matters?
  • How does knowing God affect the way we treat people?
  • What distractions compete for your attention?
  • What practical step can you take this week to pursue God more intentionally?

The Deeper Question

There is an important distinction between three stages:

STAGE 01

Information

“I know what the Bible says.”

STAGE 02

Conviction

“I believe what the Bible teaches.”

STAGE 03

Transformation

“I am allowing what I believe to change the way I live.”

The third stage is where knowledge becomes visible.

When Truth Is Tested

You know God values truth.

You believe truth matters.

Then someone offers you an opportunity to make money through deception.

Your decision reveals whether that truth has entered your life.


You know Jesus teaches forgiveness.

You believe forgiveness is important.

Then someone seriously hurts you.

Your response reveals how deeply that teaching has taken root.


You know God is sovereign.

You believe God is trustworthy.

Then your plans collapse.

Your response reveals whether your trust depends entirely on circumstances.

Final Thought

Knowing God is not simply collecting religious information.

It is a lifelong movement:

Revelation → Knowledge → Faith → Relationship → Obedience → Character → Transformation

The more we know God according to the truth He has revealed, the more that knowledge should influence the person we are becoming.

The goal is not simply to say:

“I know about God.”

The deeper goal is to be able to say:

“I am learning to know God, trust Him, walk with Him, obey Him, love what He loves, and allow His truth to transform my life.”

And that transformation is not measured only inside a church or Bible-study meeting.

It is revealed on Monday morning, when money is involved, when temptation comes, when someone disrespects you, when nobody is watching, when your plans fail, and when you have the power to choose between what is easy and what is right.
