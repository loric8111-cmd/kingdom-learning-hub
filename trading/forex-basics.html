What Is Forex?

Understand the foreign exchange market, currency pairs, participants, pricing and the basic language used by forex traders.

1. What Does Forex Mean?

Forex is short for "foreign exchange." It refers to the global marketplace where currencies are exchanged against one another.

Whenever one currency is exchanged for another, a foreign-exchange transaction takes place. Banks, businesses, governments, investors and other market participants may need to exchange currencies for different reasons.

Key idea:

Forex trading involves one currency relative to another. You are not simply buying "EUR" or "USD" in isolation—you are dealing with an exchange rate between currencies.

2. How Is Forex Quoted?

Forex is normally quoted in currency pairs. For example:

EUR/USD = 1.1000

EUR is the base currency. USD is the quote currency.

A simplified interpretation is that 1 euro is being valued at 1.1000 US dollars at that quoted exchange rate.

3. Base Currency and Quote Currency

Every forex pair contains two currencies. The first currency is the base currency and the second is the quote currency.

Pair Base Currency Quote Currency
EUR/USD EUR USD
GBP/USD GBP USD
USD/JPY USD JPY
USD/CAD USD CAD

4. Major Currency Pairs

Major currency pairs are among the most widely traded forex pairs and generally involve the US dollar paired with another major currency.

Examples include:

  • EUR/USD
  • GBP/USD
  • USD/JPY
  • USD/CHF
  • AUD/USD
  • USD/CAD
  • NZD/USD

The exact characteristics of a pair can vary with market conditions, liquidity and trading session.

5. Minor and Exotic Pairs

Not every currency pair includes the US dollar.

  • Minor pairs: Often refer to pairs involving major currencies but excluding the US dollar, such as EUR/GBP.
  • Exotic pairs: Generally involve a major currency and the currency of an emerging or smaller economy.

These pairs can have different liquidity, spreads and volatility characteristics.

6. What Moves Forex Prices?

Currency prices can change because of many factors. These include economic conditions, interest-rate expectations, monetary policy, political developments, market sentiment and changes in supply and demand.

Economic announcements can sometimes produce rapid price movements. This is one reason traders need to understand the risks associated with market volatility.

7. Who Participates in Forex?

The forex market includes many different types of participants.

  • Central banks
  • Commercial and investment banks
  • Corporations conducting international business
  • Institutional investors
  • Hedge funds and other financial institutions
  • Retail traders

8. What Are Bid and Ask Prices?

A trading platform may display two prices for a forex pair: a bid price and an ask price.

  • Bid: The price at which a market participant can generally sell to the market.
  • Ask: The price at which a market participant can generally buy from the market.

The difference between these prices is called the spread.

Simple Spread Example

Suppose EUR/USD is displayed as:

Bid: 1.1000

Ask: 1.1002

The difference is 0.0002, or 2 pips under the conventional four-decimal representation for this example.

9. What Is a Pip?

A pip is a commonly used unit for expressing small changes in the exchange rate of a currency pair.

For many major currency pairs, one pip is 0.0001. For many yen pairs, one pip is 0.01.

However, modern platforms may display additional decimal places, so you should understand the quotation convention being used by your broker.

10. Forex Sessions

Forex trading activity is commonly discussed in terms of major financial centers and their trading sessions.

Commonly referenced sessions include:

  • Sydney
  • Tokyo
  • London
  • New York

Market activity can vary throughout the day. Periods when major financial centers overlap can sometimes have greater trading activity.

11. Forex Is Not Easy Money

The accessibility of forex trading can create the false impression that making money is easy. It is not.

Prices can move unpredictably, and leverage can magnify both gains and losses. A trader can lose money even after conducting an analysis that appears reasonable.

Important:

Never treat a forex trade as guaranteed income. Learn the market, understand the risks and practice before putting real capital at risk.

12. What You Should Know Before Lesson 03

Before moving forward, make sure you understand:

  • What forex means.
  • What a currency pair represents.
  • The difference between base and quote currency.
  • The difference between major, minor and exotic pairs.
  • What bid, ask and spread mean.
  • The basic meaning of a pip.
  • Why forex prices can move.
